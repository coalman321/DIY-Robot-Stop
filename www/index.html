    Culture Club Robotics Competition 2026 Rules

    The CAT Robotics Culture Club is ready to get competitive, are you? Teams of two or more build custom vehicles and compete to earn the best score during a two-day autonomous vehicle race held at the Tech Center.

    0 - General Rules and Updates

    0.1 - IP Rules

    • Teams must not use Caterpillar Proprietary IP on vehicles.
    • Teams must use external code storage repositories like public GitHub (not GitHub Enterprise) or GitLab (not gitgis).
    • Should there be any interest in further development for Cat projects, Tech Strategists would be involved to determine usability for the 1A platform.
    • Content developed as part of this competition, or your 10% time, does not violate rule 0.1.1.
    • If there is concern about something developed being truly novel, it is recommended for competitors to avoid inclusion on their vehicles.

    0.2 - Team Rules

    • Teams must be composed of 2 or more Cat Technology or Cat Digital employees.
    • Teams may participate in a design review by June 1, 2026.
    • Teams intending to receive funding from Cat must participate in the design review and must submit a parts list by June 30.
    • Teams must demonstrate path following by August 14, 2026.
    • Teams must complete a safety review by September 25, 2026.

    0.3 - Dimensions

    • Any rule that includes dimensions will use US customary units with a metric conversion for convenience. When there is a discrepancy between the two provided units, the US customary unit value shall be considered the intended value until a correction is made.
    • All dimensions shall be considered nominal. Competition staff will ensure course construction meets all minimum or maximum dimensions. Nominal dimensions may vary ±1″ unless otherwise specified.

    1 - Safety Rules

    1.1 - General Safety

    • Robots must not include any devices or perform any actions that pose a risk to the safety of participants, volunteers, spectators, or robots.
    • Designs will be reviewed for safety in the mandatory safety review prior to the event.
    • Event staff will stop any robot deemed unsafe and will require additional inspection before it may resume operation.

    1.2 - Emergency Stop System

    • Robots must include an emergency stop system interface.
    • Each course is equipped with an emergency stop system that provides a standard wired interface teams must connect with to stop their robots.
    • Teams may provide an auxiliary emergency stop system in addition to the course-provided devices and interface.
    • The team-provided emergency stop interface must communicate with the robot wirelessly.
    • The team-provided emergency stop interface should have a communication range of at least 300 ft.
    • The team-provided emergency stop interface must not interfere with other robots.
    • The team-provided emergency stop interface must fail safe - if the robot cannot receive wireless communication signals, the robot must stop within 1 second.
    • If an emergency stop is requested, the robot must stop within 1 second.
    • Emergency stop activation devices will be located around the course and may be used by event staff and team members.
    • When an emergency stop is activated, all robot motion must cease.
    • No humans may enter the competition course perimeter unless all robots are emergency-stopped and have come to a complete stop.

    2 - Robot Rules

    2.1 - Budget

    • Teams may use up to $1000 of Caterpillar-provided funding to purchase parts.
    • Teams may use a provided perception sensor that does not count towards the $500 budget.
    • Teams may spend personal funds on their robot beyond the $500 provided by Caterpillar.

    2.2 - Robot Dimensions

    • The robot may never exceed: 16″ width × 24″ length × 16″ height.
    • The robot may not exceed 25 lbs.
    • Onboard electrical systems must never exceed 50V.

    2.3 – 2.7 - Autonomy & Operations

    • While competing, robots must not communicate with any remote control-like systems off track. Robots must operate autonomously.
    • Emergency stop system and RTK GNSS are exempt from this rule.
    • Robots must be able to autonomously start and stop.
    • Robots must start based on a visual signal on the course.
    • Robots must stop after completing the specified number of laps.

    2.8 - Identification Flag

    • All robots must include a mount for an identification flag. Event staff will provide a flag with the team number.
    • Robot must always display the team's flag when on the competition course.
    • Flag: yellow color, black numbers, 4″ height × 5″ width, steel wire pole.
    • The top of the flag must be between 24–26″ above the ground when mounted.
    • The flag mounting point on the robot may be no higher than 8″ above the ground to allow the flag to freely bend when passing through short obstacles.
    • The flag may extend beyond the robot dimensions and does not count toward weight.

    2.9 - Batteries

    • Teams must have a sufficient number of batteries to run at least 3 consecutive heats. Battery charging capability during the event may be limited.

    3 - Course Rules

    3.1 - General Course

    • The competition will be held on two courses: a speed course and an obstacle course.
    • Teams will receive a copy of the intended race course including instructions for any intersection prior to racing.
    • Both courses will guarantee minimum track width of 20″.
    • Both courses will include a vision-based starting indicator.
    • Teams may start their robot using an alternative manual trigger by taking a 5 second time penalty (exception to autonomy rules).

    3.2 - Speed Course

    • Robots will complete 3 consecutive timed laps.

    3.3 - Obstacle Course

    • Robots will complete 2 consecutive timed laps.
    • Robots may bypass obstacles by incurring a time penalty.
    • Teams wishing to bypass an obstacle must emergency stop their robot before entering the course and move their robot to the designated starting area after the bypassed obstacle.

    4 - Race Rules

    4.1 - Heats

    • Robots will race in heats.
    • Each team will receive 1 practice heat (not scored) for course calibration.
    • Teams will compete in 2–4 competition heats on each course.
    • A team's best score for each course across all heats will be used to determine winners.
    • Each heat is 10 minutes.
    • An E-Stop will be triggered when either the heat time elapses or the team declares they would like to complete their heat.
    • Within each heat, a team may attempt multiple runs. Before a scored run, the team must notify the race director so event staff can reset the course and prepare to time the run.
    • The best score from all runs within a heat is recorded for the team's heat score.
    • If a team is restricted from running on the course outside their control, the race director will either extend the heat or provide a replacement time slot.

    4.2 - E-Stop Operator

    • Teams must designate one team member to operate a remote emergency stop activator during each heat.
    • The designated person must continuously monitor the robot while it is on the course.
    • The team member may use a course-provided or team-provided auxiliary emergency stop activator.
    • The designated person must immediately activate an emergency stop when directed by event staff, the team, or whenever a dangerous situation occurs.
    • Vehicles must remain on the track until all motion ceases and the track is deemed safe by the race director.

    5 - Scoring

    Obstacle Course Penalty Formula

    Missed obstacles incur an escalating penalty added to the base time:

    Final = Base Time + 15 × (N + MAX(N−1, 0) × N / 2)

    where N = number of missed obstacles

    6 - Knowledge Sharing & Engagement

    • Teams shall use the Cat Robotics Stack Overflow to ask questions.
    • Event organizers will monitor the DIY-Robot-Challenge tag and respond to questions.
    • Teams may answer their own questions to share with other teams.
    • Rules and other documents will be archived in SharePoint.
    • Any version of documents published for viewing from other sources have no guaranteed update frequency.

    Additional Logistics

    • Teams outside the Peoria area will be given 2 spots per team to attend.
    • Teams must have at least two CAT Technology or CAT Digital employees. Other Caterpillar employees may join teams, but do not count towards the two-person minimum rule.
    • Caterpillar proprietary IP should not be used in the development of these vehicles.
    • For those interested in helping plan the event including rules and course layout, or give feedback on the preliminary rules, please contact Cole T.
